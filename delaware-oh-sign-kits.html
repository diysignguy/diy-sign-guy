Shipping & Delivery

How Your Kit Gets To Delaware

This is where ground shipping quietly beats freight. Roughly 95% of kits go out FedEx Ground, and a ground route reaches a shop on a county road off US-23 or a new building out past Sunbury every day — a rural address a freight carrier would treat as an outlying delivery is just another stop.

What shows up. Long boxes rather than a banded pallet: four welded frame sections with the LEDs already mounted inside for a standard kit, six with splice joints for a longer cabinet, sized so nothing exceeds ground limits. The power supply and mounting hardware come in with them.

Delivery day. No appointment to set, no driver calling from the road, and no gravel drive a semi will not commit to. The boxes get left, and one person can move them.

Open the boxes the day they land. Look the sections over even if the cartons look perfect, and do it before install day rather than the morning of. Photograph anything crushed, torn or bent and keep the packaging exactly as it arrived — that is what a carrier claim is built on. Finding a problem the day a box shows up is a phone call; finding it with a crew standing on a ladder is a lost day.

Where to put it. Ohio winters mean the cartons belong inside. The aluminum could wait outdoors; the power supply and LED sections in the same boxes should not sit through freezing rain. Assemble in a heated garage or shop if you have one, and let cold parts warm up first.

The exceptions go by truck. A cabinet too long to splice down into shippable sections, a pole or pylon project with structural parts that will not box, or several cabinets ordered at once are all cheaper and safer palletized — and if yours is one of those, it is priced that way in your written quote up front, not added later.
Before You Order

Measure Twice. Order Once.

Your kit is cut to a size, welded, and pre-lit before it ships, so the size has to be right the first time. In Delaware that means three phone calls before you order, not after.

1. Ask what you're allowed

Start with the City of Delaware if you are inside city limits, or Delaware County for the unincorporated townships — and the county is growing fast enough that plenty of new retail is in township land rather than in a city. Lewis Center, Powell, Sunbury and Galena each have their own rules to check. Ask for the maximum sign area and height for your zoning district, whether your sign counts as wall-mounted or freestanding, and what setback applies. Sign area is usually tied to your building frontage, so the answer is specific to your address — not to the block or to the business next door.

2. Ask your landlord

If you're in a plaza or center, the landlord's sign criteria sit on top of the city code and are often stricter. Cabinet depth, face color, letter height and mounting method are all commonly spelled out. Get the criteria sheet in writing before you pick a size — it is cheaper than rebuilding a cabinet that the center won't approve.

3. Ask about engineering and wiring

Because Ohio certifies local departments to enforce the state code, the office reviewing your drawings may be the city's or the county's depending on which side of the line you are on. Either way, a freestanding or pole sign typically needs sealed footing drawings from an Ohio-registered engineer and the lighting adds an electrical permit. Downtown Delaware's historic character can bring design review with it. Plan on a licensed electrician for the final hookup and inspection either way. We build the cabinet; the permit, the footing, the disconnect and the sign-off are local work done by local people.

Once you have your approved number, send it over and we cut to it exactly. Nothing here is legal or engineering advice — requirements change by jurisdiction and by site, so confirm yours with the City of Delaware or Delaware County, your landlord, and a licensed electrician before ordering.
Delaware Questions

Straight Answers For Delaware Businesses

How big a sign can I get as a kit?

Standard kits ship in four sections; larger signs ship in six with splice joints. Tell us your size and mounting and we quote it in writing.

Is assembly really doable without experience?

Yes — bolt-together assembly with the LEDs already mounted. Many owners do it in a garage over a weekend, or hand it to their contractor.

Am I dealing with the city or the county in Delaware County?

It depends on your exact address, and it is worth confirming first. Inside the City of Delaware you go to the city; a lot of the county's new retail sits in unincorporated township land where Delaware County handles it instead, and Lewis Center, Powell, Sunbury and Galena add their own layers. Ask before you order — the cabinet is welded to the approved size.

Who should do the electrical hookup?

A licensed electrician. Bolting the cabinet together in your own garage and hauling it out to mount is genuinely straightforward — that is the part worth doing yourself. The hookup is licensed, inspected work and these kits are not UL listed, so book the trade for install day.

We're a nationwide shop — not a local Delaware sign company — and that's exactly why the price works. DIY kits are not UL listed; please check with your local township and follow all sign, zoning, electrical, inspection and installation guidelines before ordering.
